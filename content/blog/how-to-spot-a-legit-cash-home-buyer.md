Type "sell my house fast" into Google and you'll find everything from honest
local buyers to out-of-state lead brokers who resell your information, to
outright predators hoping you won't read the contract. Here's how to tell the
difference — including the questions we think *you should ask us*.

## 1. Can you meet them?

A legitimate local buyer has a name, a face, and a phone number a real person
answers. If every conversation routes through a call center and nobody will
meet you at the property, your "buyer" may actually be a wholesaler flipping
your contract — or a lead company selling your info to the highest bidder.

## 2. Do they explain their offer?

An honest cash buyer can walk you through the math: what the house would be
worth fixed up, what the repairs cost, and what margin they need. If the
number appears out of thin air — or drops suddenly right before closing — walk
away. Last-minute price cuts are the oldest trick in this business.

## 3. Is the earnest money real?

Ask for earnest money deposited with the **title company**, not a promise. A
buyer with nothing at stake can tie your house up for weeks and vanish.

## 4. Do they use a real title company?

Closings should happen through a licensed Wisconsin title company or attorney
— never a kitchen-table signing with a notary they brought along. The title
company protects *you*: it verifies the money exists before your deed moves.

## 5. Are there fees?

You should pay **nothing** — no application fees, no "processing" charges, no
commissions. A cash buyer's offer is the number you walk away with, minus only
what you owe on the property.

## 6. What happens if you say no?

A legitimate buyer takes "no" gracefully — the offer is free and so is
declining it. High-pressure tactics, exploding deadlines measured in hours, or
scare talk about your situation are all reasons to end the conversation.

## 7. Can you verify they've actually bought houses?

Ask for addresses of recent purchases (sales are public record in Wisconsin),
references, or their title company's name. Anyone doing real business locally
can prove it in five minutes.
